Security

How SafeKeep protects your notes

Plain language, no hand-waving. What is encrypted, where the keys live, what the app can and cannot do, and where the limits are.

From your PIN to your notes

Each step unlocks the next. Break the chain anywhere and the notes stay unreadable.

Your PIN

Six digits only you know. It is never stored.

Wrapping key

Derived from the PIN with PBKDF2-SHA256.

Data key

A random 256-bit key, sealed with AES-256-GCM.

Your notes

An AES-256 encrypted database (SQLCipher).

Layer by layer

01

Notes at rest

Stored in a SQLCipher database encrypted with AES-256. The app refuses to open a database that is not encrypted, so notes can never land in a plain file by accident.

02

The data key

A random 256-bit key created on your phone. It is never written to storage in the clear.

03

PIN protection

The data key is sealed with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from your PIN, and the sealed copy is kept in Android’s Keystore-backed secure storage.

04

Fingerprint unlock

Optional. A second copy of the key is held under a Keystore key that only works after a successful fingerprint or face check.

05

Wrong PINs

Five free attempts, then a wait that doubles each time: 30 seconds, one minute, two minutes, up to an hour.

06

Backups

One file, encrypted with AES-256-GCM under a key derived from a passphrase you choose. The file is useless without it.

Closing the side doors

Encryption is not much use if notes leak around it.

No screenshots

Screenshots and the recent-apps preview are blocked by default.

Auto-lock

SafeKeep locks itself when it goes to the background.

Quiet reminders

Notifications show the note title only and are hidden on the lock screen.

No Android backup

Android’s automatic cloud backup is switched off for the app.

What the app can touch

The release build asks Android for very little. You can check this yourself in your phone’s app info screen.

It asks for

  • Notifications, for reminders
  • Alarms, so reminders fire on time
  • Fingerprint, if you turn it on
  • Vibration, for touch feedback
  • Starting after a reboot, to restore reminders
  • Screen capture alerts, which Android requires for blocking screenshots

It never asks for

  • Internet access
  • Files, photos or media
  • Contacts or call logs
  • Location
  • Camera or microphone

No internet permission. The app you download cannot open a network connection at all, so your notes cannot be uploaded anywhere, by design or by mistake.

Honest limits

What SafeKeep does not promise

There is no recovery

Forget the PIN and the only way back is an encrypted backup. Nobody, including the developer, can open your notes.

A six digit PIN is short

The protection relies on the phone’s secure storage keeping the sealed key on the device, plus the attempt limiter. On a rooted or compromised phone that is weaker.

Turning the PIN off is a trade

Notes stay encrypted on storage, but anyone who can use your unlocked phone can open the app.

Copied text leaves the vault

Copying or sharing a note hands plain text to the clipboard or to the app you chose.

No independent audit yet

The design uses standard, well-studied building blocks, but it has not been reviewed by an outside security firm, and the source code is not public at the moment.
